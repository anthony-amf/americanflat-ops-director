The number depends entirely on what you divide by

Amazon nets many different things out of a payment, and only one of them is a chargeback in the sense a CEO means it — a penalty we earned by missing a delivery commitment. The rest are contractual trade funds, claims and accounting true‑ups. Quoting a single "deduction rate" without saying which is how this KPI gets misread, so here are all three framings.

Do not quote the 53.56% as a chargeback rate

Two‑thirds of it is co‑op accruals, aged‑receivable settlements and damage allowances — periodic true‑ups covering months of trading that happened to land inside this two‑week payment window. The denominator is two weeks of invoices; the numerator is not. The ratio is arithmetically correct and economically meaningless.

Answering the direct question

Your payables → ready for deduction is the payables CSV, essentially line for line. In progress is not in either report — but it reads $0.00, so there is no hidden liability sitting behind it. The receivables side, all $3.39m of it, and the $73,657.56 of adjustments appear in neither file. To reconcile those we need the receivables and adjustments exports from behind their own View details links.

What JP should check before this goes to the CEO

  • The denominator choice. I used gross invoice face value settled in the window ($2,095,904.08). Using net-of-terms-discount ($2,062,079.38) instead moves the rate from 0.72% to 0.73% — immaterial, but it should be the same basis every period.
  • Whether a two-week window is the right reporting period. It is what the remittance export covers. A rolling quarter would be steadier and would stop periodic true-ups distorting the secondary ratios.
  • The $77,909.43 of untraceable cash. Nine payments carry remittance items Amazon will not display because the ordering codes are not attached to our account. That cash is received and banked, but cannot be tied to invoices — and it may contain further deductions that would raise the 0.72%.
  • The $317,227.20 "DFP for AR invoice". The largest un-reversed deduction in the window, and the remittance gives no explanation beyond that label. Worth confirming it was expected.
  • Whether historic chargebacks belong in the rate. $16,007.31 of chargebacks are queued but not yet taken, some dating to December 2025. Including them on an accrual basis rather than a cash basis would raise the rate to roughly 1.5%.
